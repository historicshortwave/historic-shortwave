About the Historic Shortwave Project

Why I built this

As a young shortwave listener in the 1960s and 1970s, I spent many evenings tuning across the bands, where distant and exotic stations drifted in and out through the static. It was a wonderful way to travel the world and experience other cultures without leaving home. Today those bands are mostly quiet. So many of the local stations that once filled them have moved to FM or to digital outlets, and tuning across the empty frequencies now leaves me a little sad.

Historic Shortwave is my attempt to bring back some of that experience. It plays recordings made between 1969 and 1989 on a simulated receiver, each one placed at the frequency where it was originally heard. You can tune across the 90, 60 and 49 meter bands, listen for a station identification, and then reveal the station’s details and location.

I vibe-coded this application with the help of Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 AI model.

Comments, new recordings and corrections

Comments are welcome at historicshortwave@gmail.com. If you have, or know of, other recordings that could be added to the project, please write with the details: the station, frequency, date and time, and where the recording can be found online.

If you made or shared one of these recordings and would like its credit corrected or the recording removed, please write to the same address.
