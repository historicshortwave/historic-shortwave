About the Historic Shortwave Project

Why I built this

As a young shortwave listener in the 1960’s and 1970’s I spent many evenings tuning the shortwave radio bands, where a great many distant and exotic stations came and went through the static. It was a great way to virtually travel the world and experience other cultures. Today the shortwave radio bands are mostly quiet. In particular, so many of the local stations that once filled them have moved to FM and to digital outlets, and tuning across those empty frequencies now leaves me a little sad.

Historic Shortwave is my attempt to bring back some of that original experience. It plays audio recordings made between 1960 and 1989 on a simulated receiver, each at the frequency where it was originally heard, so you can tune across the 90, 60 and 49 meter bands, listen for an identification and then reveal the station’s details and location.

Comments and new recordings

Comments are welcome at historicshortwave@gmail.com. If you have or know of other recordings that could be added to the Historic Shortwave Project, please write with the details: the station, frequency, date and time, and where the recording can be found online.

If you made or shared one of these recordings and would like its credit corrected, or would like it removed, please write to the same address.
