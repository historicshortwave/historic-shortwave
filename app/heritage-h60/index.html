HERITAGE HR-64 / TWENTY-RECORD BASELINE

FIELD NOTES / HOW TO LISTEN

A listening room, not a time machine.

Heritage HR-64 is an original GR-64-inspired interface, not a Heathkit product or endorsement. Its lettering evokes period advertising rather than reproducing the company logo. The dial and S-meter are simulated, not measured RF reception.

The BAND switch selects 90 meters (3150–3500 kHz), 60 meters (4700–5085 kHz) or 49 meters (5850–6350 kHz). Each range includes that collection's band-edge recordings; this does not redefine historical frequency allocations. Turn the BAND knob to a detent or tap a band number. Each band remembers where you last tuned. Grab and rotate the gain or tuning knob clockwise to increase its value. Fine tuning travels 12 kHz per revolution; coarse tuning travels 120 kHz. Arrow keys and the FINE step retain 0.1 kHz precision. Gain has bounded stops. Slider alternatives are available below the face.

On 60 meters, start with the twenty-record baseline or select full inventory; 90 and 49 meters always use their full 1960–1992 catalogs, with compilations, second copies and (on 49 meters) recordings outside 1960–1992 held for review. Shared frequencies use fixed clip-ID order and neutral date/clip selectors on the receiver face.

Playing recordings: Play recording starts the tuned recording, and Restart plays it again from the beginning. Playback never advances to another recording on its own, whether a recording ends or fails to load. Loop recording, between Restart and Reveal station, is off by default; its OFF/ON tag shows the setting. When it is on, the current recording starts again from the beginning each time it ends, and the line under the station title notes that Loop is on. Turning Loop on after a recording has ended does not start playback; press Replay recording. Turning it off during playback lets the recording finish its current pass and stop. Loop never moves to another recording and does not retry a recording that failed to load. The setting carries over as you tune to other recordings, and stays on until you turn it off or start a new blind session.

Static defaults to on and is controlled below the listening desk. Recorded 75-second beds and randomly timed recorded crashes follow today's date in America/New_York: December–February winter, March–May spring, June–August summer, September–November autumn. Winter is the baseline, spring/autumn are +1 dB, and summer is +2 dB. Spring temporarily uses autumn's bed and crash profile. The winter bed has mains-harmonic buzz reduction. These are seasonal impressions, not live weather or measured RF conditions.

Static is gentler during loading/buffering and fades completely out during archive playback. The recorded noise assets are bundled here; station recordings remain externally hosted. Band conditions run in the background and are not shown on the receiver face.

Noise recording credit: J. Christie / NA5B WebSDR, Washington, DC. Noise-only tails were cut from winter, summer, and autumn uploads marked Public Domain Mark 1.0 by the uploader. This label is not a blanket rights clearance for broadcast recordings.

Full mode retains all 349 entries, with existing holds, conflicting frequencies and compilation segments in a review-only queue. The twenty-record baseline is unchanged. Short clips are retained pending the owner's individual listening review.

Station names stay concealed until revealed. After a reveal, a small map highlights the transmitter's country, republic or region as its borders stood on the recording date (borders: CShapes 2.0, Schvitz et al., CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; small areas: Natural Earth). Map data is not downloaded until the first reveal. The receiver has no text boxes and keeps no log; it reads only the knobs, switches, buttons and lists on the page, and the season comes only from the date. Research buttons open the owner's original external destinations; no documents are mirrored or embedded here.

Recording credits: after you reveal a station, the line under its details names the person who made or shared the recording, using the source's own wording. "Recorded by" means the source names that person as the recordist. "Shared by" means the source says only that they contributed it. Where the source names no one, the line says so; names are never guessed. The line also gives the archive the recording comes from and, where the archive grants one, its license. The broadcasts themselves belong to the original stations.

Map credits: period borders on the Reveal Station map come from CShapes 2.0 by Schvitz, Rüegger, Girardin, Cederman, Weidmann and Gleditsch (International Conflict Research, ETH Zurich), licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
